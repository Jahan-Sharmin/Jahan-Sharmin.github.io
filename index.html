What ties my work together

Most accurate AI models are too heavy and too opaque for the places that need them most: a farm in rural Bangladesh, a low-cost CCTV box, a phone. My research asks how far a model can be compressed while staying accurate and trustworthy, and my projects put sensors, microcontrollers, databases and apps around it so it does something useful.

A Lightweight and Explainable Deep Learning Framework for Real-World Anomaly Detection on the UCF-Crime Dataset

J. Sadia, S. Jahan (second author), M. S. Akther, A. Sayed, R. Mahmud (Assistant Professor, AIUB)

Surveillance cameras far outnumber the people who can watch them, and the most accurate detectors are large "black box" models. We built a compact video anomaly detector (MobileNetV3-Small backbone with a GRU temporal head), compressed it with quantization-aware training, and measured something rarely tested: whether the model's Grad-CAM explanations survive compression.

1.2 MBfinal model size
75%smaller than FP32
0.807accuracy (unchanged)
167 FPSINT8 inference speed

Key finding: accuracy held after compression (AUC 0.888 → 0.892), but the explanations did not. FP32 and INT8 heatmaps overlapped at a mean IoU of only 0.06, so a compressed model can give the same answer for different reasons. A temporal majority-voting window also cut false alarms in simulated streams from 8 to 0.

Comparative Evaluation of Lightweight Deep Learning Architectures for Diffusion-Generated Image Detection using Edge-Oriented Optimization

U. K. Esha, S. Jahan (second author), M. J. Medha

AI-generated images are getting harder to spot, and most detectors are too heavy for phones or embedded devices. We benchmark seven architectures (MobileNetV3-Small, MobileNetV4, ShuffleNetV2, GhostNet, EfficientNetV2-S, ResNet50, DenseNet121) on Stable Diffusion v1.5 images from GenImage, in both FP32 and post-training INT8 form.

  • Metrics go beyond accuracy: F1, model size, memory, latency and throughput
  • Tests robustness to common image corruptions under simulated edge-device limits

Capstone · Ongoing (Spring 2026-27)

Integrated Multimodal IoT Platform for Real-Time Agricultural Monitoring and AI-Enabled Predictive Management

Final-year project · Team of 4 · Supervisor: Dr. Chowdhury Akram Hossain

Farmers usually monitor soil, water, weather and animals separately and by hand. We are building one modular system for crop fields, nurseries, fishponds and livestock: distributed sensors and cameras feed a processing unit that runs lightweight vision models and sends predictions, alerts and recommendations to a single mobile app.

  • Sensors: soil moisture, pH, temperature, humidity, dissolved oxygen, turbidity, plus cameras
  • Solar-charged battery nodes for remote sites; wired or wireless links to the processing unit
  • Lightweight models (MobileNet, YOLO) evaluated against task-specific models
  • Evaluated on prediction performance, response time, reliability, usability and a farmer impact survey

Smart Room System with IoT

A working scale model of a room that can be monitored and controlled from a phone. The microcontroller reads temperature and humidity, shows live status on an OLED screen, and lets the user set fan speed, switch the light and open or close the door remotely through the Blynk app. We simulated the circuit in Proteus first, then built the hardware.
